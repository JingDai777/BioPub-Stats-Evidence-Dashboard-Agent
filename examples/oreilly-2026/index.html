Daraxonrasib or Chemotherapy in Previously Treated Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer

O'Reilly EM, Wainberg ZA, Hendifar AE, et al. N Engl J Med. 2026;395:325-337. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2605555

phase 3, international, multicenter, open-label, randomized, active-controlled, parallel-group superiority trialClassification confidence: HighFull primary article; supporting files not accessible

Classification: clinical_trial. Rationale: The Methods identify a phase 3 international randomized trial in which 500 participants were prospectively assigned 1:1 to daraxonrasib or investigator-choice chemotherapy. [C-001] [C-003]

Open source publication

Executive appraisal

Reported findingIn the RAS G12 population, daraxonrasib improved overall survival (median 13.2 vs 6.6 months; HR for death 0.40, 95% CI 0.30-0.54; P<0.001) and progression-free survival (median 7.3 vs 3.5 months; HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.34-0.59; P<0.001). [C-012] [C-013]
Biostatistical interpretationThe large relative effects, confidence intervals excluding 1, concordant survival endpoints, multiplicity control, and intention-to-treat efficacy analyses support a treatment effect in the randomized RAS G12 population. Overall-survival maturity is limited because this was the first planned interim analysis, with 72 versus 127 deaths reported in the arms rather than the planned 314-death final analysis, and 8.5 months' median follow-up. [C-009] [C-020]
Primary limitationThe open-label design coincided with markedly differential treatment initiation: 97.2% of participants assigned daraxonrasib versus 84.9% assigned chemotherapy received treatment, mainly because 11.5% in the chemotherapy arm withdrew before dosing; this can affect safety and patient-reported comparisons and complicates interpretation despite intention-to-treat efficacy analysis. [C-006] [C-023]
Decision relevanceThe randomized evidence can inform treatment selection for fit adults with previously treated metastatic PDAC, particularly RAS G12 disease, while requiring caution for non-G12 tumors, patients unlike the eligibility population, and longer-term benefit-risk judgments pending mature survival follow-up and full supporting documents. [C-021] [C-022] [C-025]
Section 1

Research question and trial design

Study question
Compare daraxonrasib with investigator-choice chemotherapy in previously treated metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, with dual primary evaluation of overall survival and centrally reviewed progression-free survival in patients with RAS G12 mutations. [C-007] [C-012] [C-013]
Population
Adults with previously treated metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, measurable disease, ECOG performance-status score 0 or 1, adequate organ function, and documented RAS status. [C-002]
Sample size
500 randomized overall; 459 in the RAS G12 primary-analysis population. [C-003] [C-007]
Phase and setting
Phase 3; 59 sites in six countries; enrollment October 16, 2024 to November 7, 2025. [C-001] [C-003]
Design
International, multicenter, open-label, 1:1 randomized, active-controlled, parallel-group superiority trial; crossover was prohibited. [C-001] [C-003]
Randomization and concealment
Interactive response technology; stratified by ECOG score, metastatic disease at initial diagnosis, baseline liver metastases, and RAS status. Allocation-concealment detail beyond the system was Not reported. [C-005]
Blinding
Open-label treatment; progression-free survival and objective response were assessed by blinded independent central review under RECIST 1.1. [C-001] [C-007]
Analysis populations
Efficacy: all randomized participants (intention-to-treat operationally). Safety: all participants receiving at least one dose. [C-006] [C-007]

Treatment arms

Daraxonrasib
300 mg orally once daily; 248 assigned, 241 treated; continued until progression, unacceptable toxicity, consent withdrawal, or another stop condition. [C-003] [C-004] [C-006]
Chemotherapy
Investigator choice of gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel, modified FOLFIRINOX, FOLFOX, or liposomal irinotecan plus fluorouracil/leucovorin; 252 assigned, 214 treated; local prescribing practice. [C-003] [C-004] [C-006]

Participant flow

Randomized: 500 total; 248 daraxonrasib and 252 chemotherapy. [C-003]
Treated and safety-evaluable: 241/248 (97.2%) and 214/252 (84.9%), respectively. [C-006]
Never dosed: 7/248 (2.8%) and 38/252 (15.1%); 29 chemotherapy participants withdrew before dosing. [C-006] [C-023]
At cutoff: 105/248 (42.3%) remained on daraxonrasib and 35/252 (13.9%) remained on chemotherapy; efficacy analyses retained all randomized participants. [C-006]
Section 2

Estimand and outcome framework

Primary estimand
Not reported as a formal estimand. Operationally: randomized daraxonrasib versus randomized investigator-choice chemotherapy for time to death and time to central-review progression or death in RAS G12. [C-007] [C-008]
Primary endpoint and time point
Dual primary endpoints: overall survival and progression-free survival in RAS G12; both are time-to-event endpoints through the February 10, 2026 cutoff rather than fixed-time endpoints. [C-007] [C-011]
Endpoint ascertainment
Overall survival from follow-up; progression-free survival by blinded independent central review according to RECIST 1.1. [C-007]
Intercurrent events
Death was an event for overall survival, progression-free survival, and patient-reported time-to-deterioration endpoints. Treatment discontinuation did not remove randomized participants from efficacy analysis. Other intercurrent-event strategies and detailed censoring rules were Not reported. [C-007] [C-017]
Outcome hierarchy
RAS G12 PFS and OS were tested first under allocated alpha, followed sequentially by overall-population PFS and OS, objective response, and patient-reported endpoints in RAS G12 and then overall. Analyses outside the procedure were descriptive. [C-009]
Prespecification
Primary and key secondary endpoints and the multiplicity sequence were reported as prespecified. Subgroup categories were prespecified but the authors described subgroup interpretation as exploratory/descriptive. [C-009] [C-021]
Section 3

Statistical methodology

Hypothesis and framework
Superiority testing for daraxonrasib versus chemotherapy; two-sided familywise alpha 5%. No noninferiority or equivalence margin was reported. [C-009]
Sample-size or event rationale
OS: 314 deaths for about 84% power at alpha 4.8%, assuming HR 0.70. PFS: 235 events for about 95% power at alpha 0.2%, assuming HR 0.54. [C-010]
Primary analysis model
Kaplan-Meier estimation; stratified log-rank testing; stratified Cox model with Efron tie handling. [C-008]
Effect measure
Hazard ratio with 95% confidence interval for time-to-event contrasts, plus arm-specific Kaplan-Meier medians; arm-specific response percentages for objective response. [C-008] [C-012] [C-013] [C-016]
Covariate adjustment
Stratified by the four randomization factors. Additional covariate adjustment was Not reported. [C-005] [C-008]
Multiplicity control
Graphical procedure controlled two-sided familywise type I error at 5%; initial allocation was 4.8% to OS and 0.2% to PFS in RAS G12, with sequential propagation through key secondary endpoints. [C-009]
Missing-data handling
Not reported for detailed censoring, loss to follow-up, or patient-reported completion. Figure 1 notes survival-status ascertainment among never-treated participants, but the unavailable SAP prevents full verification. [C-006] [C-025]
Model assumptions and diagnostics
Proportional-hazards diagnostics and other Cox-model checks were Not reported. [C-008] [C-025]
Interim analyses or early stopping
Two OS interim analyses were planned. This was the first, after at least 166 RAS G12 deaths, and also the final PFS analysis. The exact interim efficacy boundary was Not accessible because the SAP was unavailable. [C-011] [C-020] [C-025]
Sensitivity and supplementary analyses
No sensitivity analyses were presented in the article; supplement/protocol/SAP analyses were Not accessible. [C-025]
Subgroup analyses
Prespecified subgroups were described as broadly consistent, but the authors emphasized that these analyses were exploratory, with small samples and wide intervals. The non-G12 subgroup had 41 participants and discordant OS/PFS point estimates. [C-021] [C-023]

Section 6

Biostatistical interpretation

Magnitude and clinical relevance
In RAS G12, HRs were 0.40 for death and 0.45 for progression or death; reported medians were 13.2 versus 6.6 months for OS and 7.3 versus 3.5 months for PFS. Patient-reported deterioration and response also favored daraxonrasib. Clinical relevance is supported by outcome breadth, but longer-term durability remains uncertain. [C-012] [C-013] [C-016] [C-017] [C-020]
Precision
Primary HR confidence intervals were comparatively narrow and excluded 1. The daraxonrasib median OS upper confidence bound was not reached. Non-G12 subgroup intervals were wide and unsuitable for definitive inference. [C-012] [C-013] [C-020] [C-021]
Robustness
Concordant OS and blinded-central-review PFS findings in RAS G12 and the overall population, plus prospective multiplicity control, support robustness. Unreported diagnostics, unavailable sensitivity analyses, and interim OS maturity temper confidence in long-term stability. [C-009] [C-012] [C-013] [C-014] [C-015] [C-020] [C-025]
Statistical versus clinical significance
The primary effects were statistically significant within the reported multiplicity procedure and large in relative and median-time terms. The article did not prespecify a minimum clinically important survival difference, so clinical importance remains a benefit-risk judgment rather than a p-value conclusion. [C-009] [C-012] [C-013]
Subgroup and exploratory evidence
Do not generalize the non-G12 estimates or other small subgroups as confirmatory. Their sample sizes and confidence intervals support descriptive hypothesis generation only. [C-021] [C-023]
Author conclusion

Among patients with previously treated metastatic PDAC, daraxonrasib led to significantly longer overall survival and progression-free survival than chemotherapy. [C-026]

Reviewer synthesis

For the randomized RAS G12 population, the trial provides persuasive evidence that daraxonrasib improved OS and centrally reviewed PFS relative to investigator-choice chemotherapy. Confidence comes from randomization, intention-to-treat efficacy analysis, concordant endpoints, and multiplicity control. The main qualifications are interim OS maturity, differential treatment initiation in an open-label setting, treated-population safety comparisons, and inaccessible supporting documents. [C-006] [C-009] [C-012] [C-013] [C-020] [C-023] [C-025]

Section 7

Limitations and applicability

  • Open-label and differential initiation: Treatment knowledge could influence reporting and treatment decisions; 15.1% assigned chemotherapy versus 2.8% assigned daraxonrasib never started treatment. [C-006] [C-023]
  • Interim survival maturity: This was the first planned OS interim analysis (72 vs 127 deaths reported in the RAS G12 arms; 314 deaths planned for final analysis) after median follow-up of 8.5 months. [C-011] [C-020]
  • Small non-G12 subgroup: Only 41 participants lacked RAS G12 mutations; OS and PFS estimates were imprecise and directionally discordant. [C-021]
  • Unavailable supporting materials: The protocol, SAP, supplement, disclosure forms, and data-sharing statement were not included in the uploaded PDF. [C-025]
  • Safety comparison: Harms were summarized among treated participants with unequal post-randomization attrition and different exposure durations, not as randomized risk estimates. [C-006] [C-018] [C-019] [C-020]
Data maturity and follow-up
Data cutoff February 10, 2026; median follow-up 8.5 months (range 3.2-15.9). At the first OS interim analysis, the RAS G12 arms had 72 versus 127 deaths; 314 deaths were planned for final analysis. PFS analysis was final. [C-011] [C-020]
Protocol deviations and adherence
Three randomized participants had baseline ECOG scores above 1 and received no treatment. No crossover was allowed. A total of 7 daraxonrasib and 38 chemotherapy participants never received treatment; efficacy analyses included all randomized participants. [C-006] [C-023]
Generalizability
Strongest for adults with measurable previously treated metastatic PDAC, ECOG 0-1, adequate organ function, and RAS G12 tumors. Applicability is uncertain for poorer performance status, CNS metastases, prior RAS-targeted therapy, heavier pretreatment, or non-G12 disease. [C-002] [C-021] [C-022]
Important context

Safety frequencies come from treated populations (241 vs 214), with exposure lasting 6.2 months for daraxonrasib versus 1.5-3.2 months across chemotherapy regimens. They should not be read as randomized risk differences. Individual conflict disclosures and full data-sharing terms were not accessible. [C-006] [C-018] [C-019] [C-024] [C-025]
