# Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses

Use this route for systematic literature reviews, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses. Use PRISMA 2020 concepts as extraction aids without claiming that reporting completeness equals review quality.

Official framework: https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020-checklist

## Classification

Identify systematic, rapid, scoping, or umbrella review; presence of meta-analysis; intervention, diagnostic, prognostic, prevalence, or etiology focus; aggregate or individual-participant data; pairwise or network meta-analysis; and update status.

## Required extraction

- Review question and eligibility framework.
- Protocol or registration and deviations.
- Databases, registers, websites, date limits, last search date, language or publication restrictions, and supplementary search methods.
- Screening process, number of reviewers, automation use, data collection, and conflict resolution.
- Selection flow: records found, deduplicated, screened, full texts assessed, exclusions with reasons, and studies or reports included.
- Included-study characteristics, populations, interventions or exposures, comparators, outcomes, follow-up, and designs.
- Risk-of-bias tool, process, and results by domain or study when available.
- Effect measure, data transformations, synthesis eligibility, model, weighting, estimator, continuity correction, and dependency handling.
- Rationale for fixed-effect, common-effect, or random-effects modeling and the estimator used for between-study variance when applicable.
- Pooled estimate, interval, study and participant counts, heterogeneity statistics, and prediction interval when reported.
- Subgroup, sensitivity, influence, meta-regression, small-study or publication-bias, and robustness analyses.
- Author-performed certainty or confidence framework and ratings.
- Funding, conflicts, data or code availability, and author limitations.

## Interpretation rules

- Separate studies from reports and participants.
- Do not invent a pooled estimate for a narrative synthesis.
- Treat heterogeneity statistics as context rather than a binary quality test.
- Interpret clinical and methodological heterogeneity alongside statistical heterogeneity.
- Do not treat a nonsignificant heterogeneity test as proof that effects are homogeneous.
- Do not infer publication bias merely from funnel-plot asymmetry or an underpowered test.
- State when certainty ratings are author-reported. Do not create GRADE ratings unless the user requests a separate appraisal.
- Do not present a pooled estimate without its outcome, time point, effect measure, model, interval, and study and participant counts when available.
- Distinguish the confidence interval for the mean pooled effect from a prediction interval for a future setting.
- Describe whether sensitivity or influence analyses materially change the conclusion.
